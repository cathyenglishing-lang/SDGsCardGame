- **A1**（25 字 / 每句 6.2 字 / 約 11.5 秒）
  > Hello everyone. Poverty is not always far away. Some classmates may skip lunch or have no quiet place to study. Today, we talk about SDG 1.
- **A2**（38 字 / 每句 12.7 字 / 約 17.5 秒）
  > Good morning. Before we talk about poverty far away, let us look around us. Poverty can be hidden in a classmate who skips lunch, a family working long hours, or a student without a quiet place to study.

  > Good morning, everyone. SDG 1 begins with one uncomfortable question: can poverty exist in a developed place like Taiwan? The answer is yes. It may be hidden, but it still shapes students' lives and futures.

### Slide 2 · SDG 1 ends poverty everywhere（消除各種形式的貧窮）

- **A1**（19 字 / 每句 9.5 字 / 約 8.8 秒）
  > SDG 1 means no poverty. People need enough money, food, a safe home, health care, and a chance to learn.
- **A2**（32 字 / 每句 10.7 字 / 約 14.8 秒）
  > SDG 1 means ending poverty in all its forms. It is not only about income. It is also about safety, health care, education, housing, and the chance to recover when life becomes difficult.
- **B1**（37 字 / 每句 12.3 字 / 約 17.1 秒）
  > SDG 1 asks countries to end poverty in every form. This includes low income, unsafe housing, weak social protection, and limited access to services. It reminds us that poverty is a system problem, not only a personal failure.

### Slide 3 · Poverty is more than money（貧窮不只是收入問題）

- **A1**（22 字 / 每句 11.0 字 / 約 10.2 秒）
  > Taiwan has many schools and technology, but some families still have a hard life. Rent, work, and health costs can be heavy.
- **A2**（36 字 / 每句 9.0 字 / 約 16.6 秒）
  > In Taiwan, many students have good schools and technology. However, not every family has the same support. Some families struggle with rent, care work, medical costs, or unstable jobs. This is why SDG 1 still matters here.
- **B1**（34 字 / 每句 11.3 字 / 約 15.7 秒）
  > Taiwan has strong education, health care, and technology, but inequality still exists. A family can look normal from outside while facing debt, care pressure, or unstable work. SDG 1 helps us notice these hidden struggles.

## Part 2 · Problem（問題指認：趨勢與因果）

語言功能：present progressive for trends / this causes ...
原表描述：Part 2: Problem Identification (Slides 4-6: Key challenges, evidence, and critical questions)

### Slide 4 · Working poverty and unstable jobs（工作貧窮與不穩定就業）

- **A1**（20 字 / 每句 10.0 字 / 約 9.2 秒）
  > Some people work hard but still do not have enough money. Unstable jobs make family life and school life harder.
- **A2**（35 字 / 每句 8.8 字 / 約 16.2 秒）
  > The first challenge is working poverty. Some people work hard but still cannot feel secure. Temporary jobs, low wages, and long hours make planning difficult. If parents are stressed, children's learning can also be affected.
- **B1**（39 字 / 每句 13.0 字 / 約 18.0 秒）
  > Working poverty is a serious problem because effort does not always lead to security. When parents face temporary contracts or low pay, they may have less time and energy to support their children. Poverty can then pass across generations.

### Slide 5 · Housing costs pressure families（居住成本加重家庭壓力）

- **A1**（21 字 / 每句 7.0 字 / 約 9.7 秒）
  > High rent can hurt families. They may have less money for food, books, or buses. A safe home helps students learn.
- **A2**（34 字 / 每句 8.5 字 / 約 15.7 秒）
  > Housing is another pressure. When rent is high, families may spend less on food, books, tutoring, or transportation. Students might move often or live in crowded rooms. A stable home is part of learning.
- **B1**（33 字 / 每句 11.0 字 / 約 15.2 秒）
  > Housing costs can quietly create poverty. If too much income goes to rent, families must cut other needs. For students, crowded housing or frequent moving can reduce sleep, concentration, and confidence at school.

### Slide 6 · Digital gaps limit opportunities（數位落差限制機會）

- **A1**（16 字 / 每句 5.3 字 / 約 7.4 秒）
  > Some students have computers and good internet. Some only have a phone. This makes learning unfair.
- **A2**（30 字 / 每句 7.5 字 / 約 13.8 秒）
  > Poverty also appears online. Some students have fast internet, laptops, and parents who can help. Others share one phone or cannot join online learning easily. Digital gaps become learning gaps.
- **B1**（32 字 / 每句 10.7 字 / 約 14.8 秒）
  > Digital access is now part of basic opportunity. Without a reliable device, internet connection, and support, students cannot fully join homework, research, or AI learning. Poverty becomes less visible, but more powerful.

## Part 3 · Achievements（成果：舉證與比較）

語言功能：present perfect / comparatives
原表描述：Part 3: Achievements and Progress (Slides 7-9: What Taiwan has done well, with scrutiny)

### Slide 7 · Safety nets protect basic needs（社會安全網守住基本生活）

- **A1**（21 字 / 每句 10.5 字 / 約 9.7 秒）
  > Taiwan has help for poor families, health care, and emergency support. These programs protect people, but they must reach people early.
- **A2**（34 字 / 每句 11.3 字 / 約 15.7 秒）
  > Taiwan has important safety nets, such as social assistance, health insurance, emergency help, and local services. These systems protect basic needs. Still, the hardest question is whether support reaches people before problems become crises.
- **B1**（37 字 / 每句 12.3 字 / 約 17.1 秒）
  > Taiwan has built several safety nets, including health insurance, social welfare, emergency relief, and local service centers. These protect many people. However, good policy must be easy to find, simple to apply for, and respectful to use.

### Slide 8 · Schools and meals support children（校園支持與營養照顧）

- **A1**（19 字 / 每句 9.5 字 / 約 8.8 秒）
  > Schools can help children. Free meals, scholarships, teachers, and after-school help make students feel safe and ready to learn.
- **A2**（34 字 / 每句 8.5 字 / 約 15.7 秒）
  > Schools can reduce poverty's effect on children. Meal programs, scholarships, counseling, after-school support, and teachers who notice students early are all important. School is not only a classroom. It can be a support system.
- **B1**（35 字 / 每句 11.7 字 / 約 16.2 秒）
  > Schools are powerful because they see students every day. Meal support, scholarships, counseling, and after-school programs can reduce pressure on families. When teachers notice needs early, school becomes a bridge between students and community resources.

### Slide 9 · Community groups fill service gaps（社區組織補足服務缺口）

- **A1**（21 字 / 每句 7.0 字 / 約 9.7 秒）
  > Community groups can give food, care, and help. But help should not only be gifts. It should build confidence and skills.
- **A2**（34 字 / 每句 8.5 字 / 約 15.7 秒）
  > Community groups also help. Food banks, social workers, religious groups, NGOs, and volunteer teams can find needs that official systems miss. But charity alone is not enough. Long-term support must build skills and dignity.
- **B1**（33 字 / 每句 8.2 字 / 約 15.2 秒）
  > Community organizations often understand local problems deeply. They may provide food, tutoring, care, or job information. Yet SDG 1 is not just charity. The goal is to help people gain choices, skills, and dignity.

## Part 4 · Solutions（解方：做法與取捨）

### Slide 10 · Financial literacy builds resilience（理財素養培養韌性）

- **A1**（17 字 / 每句 8.5 字 / 約 7.8 秒）
  > Students can learn how to budget, save, and avoid scams. Money skills help us make safer choices.
- **A2**（35 字 / 每句 11.7 字 / 約 16.2 秒）

- **B1**（33 字 / 每句 11.0 字 / 約 15.2 秒）
  > Financial literacy gives students practical power. By learning budgets, savings, debt, insurance, and scams, young people can make better decisions. Of course, education cannot replace fair wages, but it can strengthen personal resilience.

### Slide 11 · Data maps find hidden needs（數據地圖看見隱性需求）

- **A1**（17 字 / 每句 8.5 字 / 約 7.8 秒）
  > Technology can help us see needs. Students can make maps about food, buses, care, or learning support.
- **A2**（32 字 / 每句 10.7 字 / 約 14.8 秒）
  > Technology can also help. Students can use simple data maps to understand where help is needed: food access, public transportation, elderly care, or after-school programs. Data should guide kindness, not replace it.
- **B1**（35 字 / 每句 8.8 字 / 約 16.2 秒）
  > Data tools can reveal hidden poverty. For example, students can map service locations, transportation gaps, or after-school resources. Used carefully, data helps communities make smarter decisions. But every data point is still a real person.

### Slide 12 · Service learning connects action（服務學習連結真實行動）

- **A1**（16 字 / 每句 8.0 字 / 約 7.4 秒）

- **B1**（35 字 / 每句 11.7 字 / 約 16.2 秒）
  > Service learning turns SDG 1 into real experience. Students can interview social workers, create bilingual resources, or support tutoring programs. This also improves English because students speak for a real purpose, not only for a grade.

## Part 5 · Implications（影響：推論後果）

語言功能：first conditional / however
原表描述：Part 5: Implications and Future Challenges (Slides 13-16: Broader impacts and debates)

### Slide 13 · Aging society raises poverty risk（高齡社會提高貧窮風險）

- **A1**（16 字 / 每句 8.0 字 / 約 7.4 秒）
  > Taiwan has more older people. Some live alone and need money, care, buses, and digital help.
- **A2**（33 字 / 每句 11.0 字 / 約 15.2 秒）
  > Future poverty may grow with aging. Older people living alone may need care, money, transport, and digital support. Young people should understand that poverty is connected to health, family structure, and public services.
- **B1**（32 字 / 每句 10.7 字 / 約 14.8 秒）
  > An aging society changes poverty. Some older adults may live alone, have health problems, or struggle with digital services. SDG 1 therefore connects welfare, transportation, health care, and community relationships, not just income.

### Slide 14 · Migrant families need fair access（移民家庭需要公平資源）

- **A1**（17 字 / 每句 8.5 字 / 約 7.8 秒）
  > Migrant workers and new immigrant families may face language problems. Fair support helps them live and learn.
- **A2**（33 字 / 每句 11.0 字 / 約 15.2 秒）
  > Another challenge is fair access for migrant workers and new immigrant families. Language, documents, and cultural differences can make services hard to use. Inclusive support helps children and adults participate fully in society.
- **B1**（34 字 / 每句 11.3 字 / 約 15.7 秒）
  > Poverty can be harder for migrant workers and new immigrant families because language and paperwork create barriers. If services are confusing, people may not receive help. Inclusion means making information clear, multilingual, and respectful.

### Slide 15 · Climate shocks hurt vulnerable groups（氣候衝擊傷害弱勢群體）

- **A1**（19 字 / 每句 6.3 字 / 約 8.8 秒）
  > Floods and hot weather hurt poor families more. They have less money to recover. Climate action also fights poverty.
- **A2**（32 字 / 每句 10.7 字 / 約 14.8 秒）
  > Climate disasters can also deepen poverty. Floods, heat, and extreme weather hurt low-income families first because they have fewer savings and fewer choices. SDG 1 is connected to climate action and disaster preparation.
- **B1**（33 字 / 每句 11.0 字 / 約 15.2 秒）
  > Climate risk is a poverty issue. When disasters happen, low-income families often lose more and recover more slowly. Preparing shelters, insurance, public information, and community networks can protect vulnerable people before emergencies arrive.

### Slide 16 · Dignity changes how we help（用尊嚴改變援助方式）

- **A1**（15 字 / 每句 5.0 字 / 約 6.9 秒）
  > People need help with respect. Poverty is not shame. We should listen and work together.
- **A2**（33 字 / 每句 8.2 字 / 約 15.2 秒）
  > Finally, we must talk about dignity. Helping people should not make them feel ashamed. If we only see poor people as weak, we miss their strengths. Real support listens, respects, and works together.
- **B1**（35 字 / 每句 8.8 字 / 約 16.2 秒）
  > Dignity changes the way we help. Poverty should not be treated as shame or laziness. Many people are strong but face unfair conditions. Respectful support asks people what they need and includes them in decisions.
