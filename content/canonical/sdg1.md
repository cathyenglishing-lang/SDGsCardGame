## Part 1 · Introduction（引言：破題與定義）

- **B1**（35 字 / 每句 11.7 字 / 19.1 秒 ·（本版新寫））
  > SDG 1 opens with an uncomfortable question. Can real poverty survive inside a developed society like Taiwan? It can, and it quietly decides how far a young person in this room is able to go.
- **B2**（33 字 / 每句 16.5 字 / 16.5 秒 ·（本版新寫））
  > Prosperity is remarkably good at hiding hardship, which is why my first question is uncomfortable. Does poverty still exist in a developed Taiwan, or have we simply been trained not to notice it?

### 2. SDG 1 ends poverty everywhere（消除各種形式的貧窮）

- **A2**（31 字 / 每句 10.3 字 / 19.6 秒 ·（本版新寫））
  > SDG 1 asks every country to end poverty in all its forms. That is not only about income. It is health care, housing, education, and a fair chance to start again.
- **B1**（34 字 / 每句 11.3 字 / 18.5 秒 ·（本版新寫））
  > SDG 1 asks governments to end poverty in every form. That includes low income, unsafe housing and weak social protection. The goal treats poverty as a fault in the system, not a personal failure.
- **B2**（39 字 / 每句 19.5 字 / 19.5 秒 ·（本版新寫））
  > Ending poverty in all of its forms is the first global goal, and that wording deliberately reaches past household income. It covers housing, protection and access, so poverty is framed as a structural condition rather than a personal fault.

### 3. Poverty is more than money（貧窮不只是收入問題）

- **A2**（30 字 / 每句 10.0 字 / 18.9 秒 ·（本版新寫））

- **B1**（34 字 / 每句 11.3 字 / 18.5 秒 ·（本版新寫））
  > Taiwan has strong education, health care and technology. Even so, inequality remains. A family can look normal from outside while carrying debt, nursing a sick parent, or living on work that could end tomorrow.
- **B2**（38 字 / 每句 19.0 字 / 19.0 秒 ·（本版新寫））

## Part 2 · Problem（問題指認：趨勢與因果）

語言功能：present progressive for trends / this causes ...

### 4. Working poverty and unstable jobs（工作貧窮與不穩定就業）

- **A2**（31 字 / 每句 10.3 字 / 19.6 秒 ·（本版新寫））
  > The first problem is working poverty. People work hard and still feel unsafe. Short contracts, low pay and long hours break their plans, and tired parents lose time with their children.
- **B1**（36 字 / 每句 12.0 字 / 19.6 秒 ·（本版新寫））
  > Working poverty matters because hard work no longer brings security. Parents on temporary contracts lose the hours and the energy their children need. Poverty then travels into the next generation, and that is how it repeats.
- **B2**（36 字 / 每句 18.0 字 / 18.0 秒 ·（本版新寫））
  > Working poverty unsettles us because it breaks the promise that effort brings security. Parents trapped in temporary contracts give up the attention their children need, and the disadvantage is passed on, quietly, to the next generation.

### 5. Housing costs pressure families（居住成本加重家庭壓力）

- **A2**（31 字 / 每句 10.3 字 / 19.6 秒 ·（本版新寫））
  > Rent is the next weight. When it is high, families spend less on food, books and tutoring. Some students move house often or share one crowded room, where study is hard.
- **B1**（34 字 / 每句 11.3 字 / 18.5 秒 ·（本版新寫））
  > Housing costs create poverty quietly. Once the rent takes too much of the income, everything else has to be sacrificed. Students who move often or share a crowded room lose sleep, concentration and confidence.
- **B2**（35 字 / 每句 17.5 字 / 17.5 秒 ·（本版新寫））
  > What an unaffordable rent removes is not simply money but everything that money would otherwise have purchased. Teenagers who relocate annually, or study on a bunk bed, reach school short of sleep, concentration and self-belief.

### 6. Digital gaps limit opportunities（數位落差限制機會）

- **A2**（31 字 / 每句 10.3 字 / 19.6 秒 ·（本版新寫））
  > Poverty also shows up online. Some students have fast internet, a laptop and parents who can help. Others share one phone, so a digital gap slowly turns into a learning gap.
- **B1**（36 字 / 每句 12.0 字 / 19.6 秒 ·（本版新寫））
  > Digital access is now part of basic opportunity. Without a reliable device, a good connection and someone to ask, a student cannot fully join homework, research or AI learning. Their poverty is simply harder to see.
- **B2**（34 字 / 每句 17.0 字 / 17.0 秒 ·（本版新寫））
  > Digital participation has become a precondition for opportunity rather than a convenience. A student without a working device, a stable connection and help at home is shut out of homework, research and artificial intelligence.

## Part 3 · Achievements（成果：舉證與比較）

- **B2**（39 字 / 每句 19.5 字 / 19.5 秒 ·（本版新寫））

### 9. Community groups fill service gaps（社區組織補足服務缺口）

- **B1**（36 字 / 每句 12.0 字 / 19.6 秒 ·（本版新寫））

- **B2**（37 字 / 每句 18.5 字 / 18.5 秒 ·（本版新寫））

## Part 4 · Solutions（解方：做法與取捨）

### 12. Service learning connects action（服務學習連結真實行動）

## Part 5 · Implications（影響：推論後果）

語言功能：first conditional / however

### 13. Aging society raises poverty risk（高齡社會提高貧窮風險）

- **A2**（28 字 / 每句 9.3 字 / 17.7 秒 ·（本版新寫））
  > Taiwan is growing older fast. Many older people live alone and need care, money, transport, and help with their phone. Poverty follows health and family, not only pay.
- **B1**（36 字 / 每句 12.0 字 / 19.6 秒 ·（本版新寫））
  > An aging society changes the shape of poverty. Older adults may live alone, manage illness, or struggle with services that have moved online. SDG 1 therefore connects welfare, transport, health care and community, not just income.
- **B2**（38 字 / 每句 19.0 字 / 19.0 秒 ·（本版新寫））
  > Demographic change is moving poverty risk onto the oldest: anyone living alone, managing a chronic illness, or stranded when the counter became an app. The goal therefore reaches into pensions, buses, clinics and neighborly contact, not wages alone.

### 14. Migrant families need fair access（移民家庭需要公平資源）

- **A2**（31 字 / 每句 10.3 字 / 19.6 秒 ·（本版新寫））
  > Migrant workers and new immigrant families need a fair door in. Language, forms and customs make help hard to reach. When a service is clear, the whole family can use it.
- **B1**（34 字 / 每句 11.3 字 / 18.5 秒 ·（本版新寫））
  > Poverty is harder to escape for migrant workers and new immigrant families. Language and paperwork can build a wall around help. If the message is clear and multilingual, more people will ask for it.
- **B2**（38 字 / 每句 19.0 字 / 19.0 秒 ·（本版新寫））
  > For migrant workers and new immigrant families, entitlement and access are split by language, paperwork and official habit. Help that cannot be understood is help that does not exist, so inclusion has to be clear, multilingual and respectful.

### 15. Climate shocks hurt vulnerable groups（氣候衝擊傷害弱勢群體）

- **A2**（29 字 / 每句 9.7 字 / 18.3 秒 ·（本版新寫））
  > Climate disasters make poverty worse. Floods and heat hit low-income homes hardest, because they have less money saved and fewer ways out. Climate action is part of this fight.
- **B1**（36 字 / 每句 12.0 字 / 19.6 秒 ·（本版新寫））
  > Climate risk is a poverty issue. When a disaster arrives, low-income families lose more and recover far more slowly. So shelters, insurance, clear advice and neighbors who check on each other have to be ready first.
- **B2**（37 字 / 每句 18.5 字 / 18.5 秒 ·（本版新寫））
  > Climate change acts as an inequality multiplier, because the same flood hits two homes very differently, depending on savings and insurance. Adaptation is poverty policy, and shelters, advice and local networks must exist before the emergency arrives.

### 16. Dignity changes how we help（用尊嚴改變援助方式）

- **A2**（28 字 / 每句 9.3 字 / 17.7 秒 ·（本版新寫））
  > Now, dignity. Help should never make a person feel small. If we only see weakness, we miss the strength that is already there, so we should listen first.
- **B1**（34 字 / 每句 11.3 字 / 18.5 秒 ·（本版新寫））
  > Dignity changes the way support is given. Poverty should never be treated as shame or laziness, because many people are strong and simply face unfair conditions. Respectful help asks people what they actually need.
- **B2**（39 字 / 每句 19.5 字 / 19.5 秒 ·（本版新寫））
  > Dignity deserves treatment as a design principle rather than a courtesy, since help delivered with contempt is often refused. People in poverty are disadvantaged rather than lacking, and support built with them works better than support built for them.

## Part 6 · Call to action（結語：總結與號召）

- **B1**（36 字 / 每句 12.0 字 / 19.6 秒 ·（本版新寫））

- **B2**（38 字 / 每句 19.0 字 / 19.0 秒 ·（本版新寫））

### 18. Challenge blame and stereotypes（挑戰責怪與刻板印象）

- **B1**（36 字 / 每句 12.0 字 / 19.6 秒 ·（本版新寫））

- **B2**（39 字 / 每句 19.5 字 / 19.5 秒 ·（本版新寫））
  > Perhaps the most powerful reform is a change of vocabulary, since the word lazy closes an argument that evidence should open. Replace it with a question about obstacles, and suddenly you are debating pay, rent, clinics, buses and schools.
