Here is the external examiner review of the Pecha Kucha material.

### 1. LEVEL VALIDITY

**A2 Level**
* **Valid:** The level successfully relies on high-frequency verbs, basic past/present simple structures, and simple coordinators (*and, so, but*).
* **Failures:** 
    * **Slide 4:** *"while the whole world warmed by 1.3."* Contrastive *while* is a B1 feature; an A2 learner would use *but*.
    * **Slide 10:** *"minute by minute."* A2 learners process single adverbs of frequency, not reduplicated temporal idioms.
    * **Slide 12:** *"a battery you can swap"*. The omission of the relative pronoun (*that*) is a B1 syntactic feature. A2 learners either insert *that* or avoid the structure entirely.
    * **Slide 16:** *"Watch out for greenwashing."* A2 learners lack the morphological awareness to decode a portmanteau like *greenwashing* unless explicitly pre-taught. Furthermore, *watch out for* is a B1 phrasal verb.

**B1 Level**
* **Valid:** Appropriate use of the present perfect for recent changes (Slide 1: *"Have you noticed how strange our weather has become?"*) and relative clauses (Slide 3: *"The industries that pay..."*).
* **Failures:**
    * **Slide 1:** *"...mark the leading edge of a climate emergency."* *Leading edge* is a C1 idiomatic noun phrase. A B1 learner cannot naturally produce this.
    * **Slide 5:** *"...chip plants that anchor our economy."* Using *anchor* as a metaphorical verb is B2/C1.
    * **Slide 18:** *"...and those choices will outlast every politician."* *Outlast* as a transitive prefix-verb is B2.

**B2 Level**
* **Valid:** Appropriate use of reduced relative clauses (Slide 12: *"scooters manufactured here"*) and more complex passive structures.
* **Failures:** The B2 text consistently fails because it has no B2 spoken grammatical features (e.g., cleft sentences, fronting, complex conditionals, varied participle clauses, modal perfects). Instead, it achieves complexity solely through C1/C2 academic lexical insertion.
    * **Slide 6:** *"which generation absorbs the debt we are quietly building?"* There is no B2 grammatical feature here; it is a B1 syntactic structure carrying a C1 metaphorical collocation (*absorbs the debt*).
    * **Slide 19:** *"Despair is analytically useless here, whereas curiosity is genuinely productive."* This is not B2; this is C2 written academic prose. No B2 learner would spontaneously utter this in a semi-scripted presentation.

### 2. PROGRESSION

The progression from A2 to B1 to B2 is largely an illusion. Rather than representing an increase in linguistic or syntactic demand, the text repeatedly relies on a find-and-replace thesaurus swap. The levels are pedagogically identical on the following slides:

* **Slide 1 (B1/B2):** Identical present perfect/present simple structure. (*"Have you noticed..."* vs *"Something has shifted..."*).
* **Slide 2 (B1/B2):** Identical verb complementation. (*"asks every government to cut carbon and prepare"* vs *"asks every government to reduce emissions and to build"*).
* **Slide 4 (B1/B2):** Identical imperative + prepositional phrase. (*"Start with the measurements..."* vs *"Consider the measurements..."*).
* **Slide 6 (B1/B2):** Identical first conditional. (*"If industrial demand keeps rising, can clean power ever catch up?"* vs *"If industrial demand keeps climbing, can clean power ever really overtake it"*).
* **Slide 7 (B1/B2):** Merely swapping the active subject. (*"Taiwan passed... which wrote..."* vs *"The 2023 Act wrote..."*).
* **Slide 13 (B1/B2):** Same syntactic role for the adverbial. (*"Cutting carbon is expensive, though..."* vs *"Cutting carbon nevertheless imposes..."*).
* **Slide 18 (B1/B2):** Exact same syntax. (*"...will outlast every politician now in office"* vs *"...will outlive every administration now in office"*).

### 3. DISCOURSE AS ONE SPEECH

The six-part Pecha Kucha structure is barely audible because the "implications" section is a disjointed mess. 

* **The thread breaks at Slide 13:** Slides 1-12 follow a logical path: problem (warming, grid) to solutions (wind, solar, carbon capture, scooters). Slide 13 then abruptly pivots to small business costs. Slide 14 jumps to national energy security (nuclear vs gas). Slide 15 jumps to international relations. Slide 16 jumps to corporate greenwashing. These four slides read like a bulleted list of leftover climate themes rather than a cohesive rhetorical arc leading to the summary.
* **Repetition/Contradiction:** Scooters appear twice and undermine the argument. Slide 9 frames them as a habit problem (*"yet scooters still fill our roads"*), but Slide 12 frames them as a national solution (*"Taiwan makes electric scooters with a battery you can swap"*).
* **Weakest slide:** Slide 16. Greenwashing is introduced abruptly as a brand new concept 80 seconds before the end of the speech. It does not follow from international relations (Slide 15) and does not naturally lead into the summary (Slide 17). It destroys the pacing and thematic scale.

### 4. NATURALNESS

**Register Slips and Awkward Constructions:**
* **Slide 2, A2:** *"carbon gas"* -> Unnatural. **Correction:** *"carbon emissions"* or *"pollution"*.
* **Slide 3, B2:** *"an export-driven economy built around power-hungry manufacturing that never stops."* -> Too dense for a spoken breath group. **Correction:** *"an export economy relying on manufacturing that never stops."*
* **Slide 5, B2:** *"Our difficulty is structural rather than moral."* -> Pretentious and essayistic. **Correction:** *"The problem is built into the system."*
* **Slide 8, B1:** *"factory estates"* -> Unidiomatic. **Correction:** *"industrial parks"*.
* **Slide 8, B2:** *"aquaculture ponds"* -> Written register. **Correction:** *"fish farms"*.
* **Slide 11, B1:** *"proves the chemistry"* -> Unidiomatic. **Correction:** *"proves the technology works"*.
* **Slide 11, B2:** *"validates the chemistry"* -> Unidiomatic. **Correction:** *"proves the concept"*.
* **Slide 11, B2:** *"proportion of this work"* -> Unnatural phrasing. **Correction:** *"scale of the challenge"*.
* **Slide 12, B1:** *"Our streets carry a different kind of idea."* -> Bizarre phrasing. **Correction:** *"Our streets show a different approach."*
* **Slide 13, B1:** *"carry those firms"* -> Unidiomatic in this context. **Correction:** *"support those firms"*.
* **Slide 13, B2:** *"cleaner gear"* -> *Gear* is too informal for the otherwise highly academic tone of B2. **Correction:** *"cleaner equipment"*.
* **Slide 15, B2:** *"The atmosphere ignores sovereignty entirely."* -> Laughably pompous for a student speech. **Correction:** *"The climate doesn't care about borders."*
* **Slide 16, B2:** *"Scepticism is a civic skill worth practising."* -> Written register. **Correction:** *"We need to remain critical."*
* **Slide 19, B1:** *"Fear is not a useful answer to this."* -> **Correction:** *"Fear won't help us."*
* **Slide 19, B2:** *"Despair is analytically useless here, whereas curiosity is genuinely productive."* -> Academic robot speak. **Correction:** *"Giving up won't solve this, but staying curious will."*
* **Slide 19, B2:** *"communicate whatever you establish"* -> Unnatural. **Correction:** *"share what you learn"*.
* **Slide 20, B2:** *"Which returns the question to this room:"* -> Grammatical fragment. **Correction:** *"This brings the question back to this room:"*

### 5. DELIVERABILITY

L1 Mandarin speakers will struggle severely with consonant clusters, shifting stress, and the sheer syllabic density required to fit these lines into a strict 20-second window.

**A2 Hardest Lines:**
1. **Slide 4:** *"world warmed by 1.6 degrees"* (Students will swallow the past *-ed* before the *by* and struggle with the /rld/ cluster).
2. **Slide 7:** *"passed a climate law"* (The /st/ followed by a schwa requires linking they have not mastered).
3. **Slide 11:** *"catch about 2,000 tonnes of carbon each year."* (Rhythmic pacing over the number parsing, combined with the affricate /tʃ/ boundary at *catch about*).

**B1 Hardest Lines:**
1. **Slide 7:** *"Climate Change Response Act"* (The /stʃ r/ transition and the final /kt/ cluster will collapse their pacing entirely).
2. **Slide 11:** *"demonstration unit at the Taichung power station"* (Demands mastery of polysyllabic stress-timing to fit in the time limit).
3. **Slide 3:** *"industries that pay our bills"* (Consonant cluster in *industries*, and linking the plural /z/ into the /ð/).

**B2 Hardest Lines:**
1. **Slide 19:** *"analytically useless here, whereas curiosity is genuinely productive"* (Multiple five-syllable words with shifting stress—*anaLYtically* vs *curiOSity*. Impossible to articulate cleanly in 20 seconds).
2. **Slide 5:** *"chip industry that underwrites our prosperity"* (Stress shifts to *prosPERity*, alongside the /ts/ to /aʊ/ link in *underwrites our*).
3. **Slide 7:** *"bills the largest emitters for what they release"* (Linking the /st/ in *largest* smoothly to *emitters*).

### 6. FACTUAL RISK

* **Slide 4 (Global Warming):** *"Taiwan warmed by 1.6 degrees while the whole world warmed by 1.3."* Stating global warming is at 1.3 is out of date (current WMO/IPCC figures place it around 1.45+). **Correction:** Update to 1.45, or use a phrase like *"the historical global average"*.
* **Slide 7 (Carbon Fee):** *"A carbon fee introduced in 2025 now bills the largest emitters".* Taiwan's carbon fee implementation is currently delayed and politically contentious. Claiming it *"now bills"* is a factual risk. **Correction:** Use *"is set to bill"* or *"will bill"*.
* **Slide 14 (Nuclear vs Gas):** *"forcing a hard choice between more gas and a nuclear restart."* Framing a nuclear restart as an unavoidable binary choice is highly politicised in Taiwan. Raising this in an undergraduate classroom risks alienating students or igniting a partisan debate the teacher is unequipped for. **Correction:** *"forcing us to rethink how we guarantee stable power."*
* **Slide 15 (UN Exclusion):** *"though we cannot join the United Nations"* / *"excluded from the United Nations."* Explicitly mentioning UN exclusion in a mixed-nationality university classroom (which will likely include PRC students) is an unnecessary geopolitical risk for an EFL lesson. **Correction:** *"though we are excluded from major international climate frameworks."*

### 7. VERDICT

This text is not usable as a calibration standard in its current form. While it passes machine readability gates, it fails entirely as an authentic human speech. The progression from B1 to B2 is largely an illusion created by substituting conversational vocabulary with C1 academic written phrasing, resulting in a B2 script that sounds like a pompous journal article rather than a Pecha Kucha presentation. Furthermore, the narrative structure collapses in the final third, devolving into a disjointed list of unrelated themes. Before this can be used to benchmark other missions, you must: 1) rewrite the B2 level to utilize genuine spoken grammatical complexity (e.g., cleft sentences, fronting) rather than academic lexis; 2) rewrite Slides 13-16 to create a logical rhetorical bridge from 'solutions' to the 'call to action'; and 3) remove the politically inflammatory binary on nuclear power and the overt mention of UN exclusion.
